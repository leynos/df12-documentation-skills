# Capture-path evidence correction – technical design amendment

Status: Draft – two verification claims withdrawn pending evidence.

Audience: Design reviewers, the implementation lead, and the author of the
receiving ExecPlan.

Companion documents: `capture-path-evidence-execplan.md`, the execution
contract that discharges this amendment. No terms of reference, architecture
decision record (ADR), roadmap, or technical design revision was available in
the authoring environment; see Conformance basis.

Date and version: 2026-11-19, version 0.1.

Authority and evidence basis: The findings below come from the task report
supplied for this review. They were not reproduced by running the repository,
because the authoring environment had no access to the affected code, tests, or
benchmark. The report is evidence that the described conditions were observed.
The mechanistic explanations in this amendment are claims the ExecPlan must
verify; they are not established by the report.

## Scope and decision boundary

This amendment corrects two verification claims in the capture-path design.
It does not change problem-space intent, user-facing behaviour, or the agreed
performance target. No upstream intent document was available, so no
problem-space claim is altered and none is invented.

The amendment is deliberately narrow:

1. It records that the multi-chunk test passes while exercising one iteration
   at the larger chunk size, so the pass does not establish multi-chunk
   coverage.
2. It records that the benchmark measures an echo path while the design claims
   a capture-only gain, so the measurement does not establish that claim.

It does not assert that the capture-only gain is false, and it does not assert
that multi-chunk behaviour is broken. Both propositions are unverified. The
evidence is insufficient, not contradictory.

## Baseline evidence

E1 – Multi-chunk test. The test named for multi-chunk behaviour passes after
the chunk size was increased. Under the reported configuration the capture loop
executes one iteration. The test therefore passes without exercising the
behaviour in its name. Status: reported observation, not rerun here.

E2 – Performance benchmark. The benchmark measures an echo workload. This
amendment uses "echo" to mean the path that captures input and then replays or
returns it; the ExecPlan must confirm the actual composition against the code.
The design attributes the measured result to a capture-only change. The
measured execution path is not the claimed path. Status: reported observation,
not rerun here.

Both findings are about evidence scope. Neither is a new functional defect
report.

## Multi-chunk coverage model

Let `C` be the chunk size in bytes and `L` the payload length in bytes. For a
reader that fills a buffer and then processes it, the number of loop entries
`n` is at least `ceil(L / C)` when reads are full, and may be greater when
short reads occur. A test is non-vacuous for multi-chunk behaviour only if the
observed `n` is at least two and at least one chunk boundary is crossed.

In the reported case, increasing `C` without increasing `L` moved the test into
the region where `L <= C`, so `n = 1`. The test still passed, which means its
assertions do not require more than one iteration, or are satisfied vacuously.
The corrective assertion must use a harness-owned observation of loop entries
or reads, not a count derived inside the code under test. A count derived from
the same loop it is meant to audit would be circular.

Residual uncertainty: short reads, buffering above the reader, and an extra
end-of-file read can change `n`. The tests therefore assert a lower bound of
two for a payload strictly larger than two chunks, and assert the exact
observed count only where the harness controls the read pattern.

### Lemmas and axioms

`L-VER-001`: if the harness observes at least two loop entries, then at least
one iteration processed a chunk after the first read, so the multi-chunk path
was exercised. This depends on the reader structure recorded in ExecPlan
`EP-M0`.

`L-EVID-001`: if the path selector is capture-only, the echo invocation count
is zero, and captured bytes are positive, then the measured sample did not
execute the echo stage. This depends on instrumentation accuracy.

`L-EVID-002`: a difference measured between echo and baseline is not evidence
of a capture-only difference, because the two paths differ by the echo stage.
This is established by path construction, not by measurement.

`AXIOM-INSTR`: the harness counters record actual invocations and bytes.

`AXIOM-TIMER`: the benchmark's timer measures the stated resource with
documented resolution.

`AXIOM-ENV`: the recorded environment is stable within the reported bounds.

`AXIOM-READER`: one loop body execution corresponds to one successful read.
`EP-M0` must confirm this against the code, or the model above is revised
before `EP-M1` proceeds.

## Decision record

`DR-1`: Do not accept passing results as discharge. The multi-chunk test pass
and the benchmark result are real observations, but they do not exercise the
claimed properties. Accepting them would make verification vacuous.

`DR-2`: Record `TDD-PERF-001` as unverified, not falsified. The benchmark is
out of scope for the claim; it does not contradict a capture-only gain.

`DR-3`: Do not lower the acceptance threshold. If the capture-only measurement
falls short, the claim is revised or abandoned by its owner.

`DR-4`: Withdraw `TDD-DOC-001` from the design until `OBL-EVID-001` and
`OBL-EVID-002` are discharged and `OBL-DOC-001` is reconciled.

`DR-5`: No formal proof or model checking is proportionate. The obligations are
finite boundary coverage and instrumented measurement, not protocol or
arithmetic theorems.

## Compatibility assessment

No public interface, persisted format, or wire format changes are involved.
The instrumentation is a test-only surface and creates no source-API
compatibility obligation. No compatibility layer is authorised.

## Freshness and evidence expiry

The evidence in this amendment expires on any of the following:

1. A change to the chunk size, fixture length, or read loop.
2. A change to the benchmark entrypoint, input generation, or timing
   protocol.
3. A rebase or upstream merge that moves the test, benchmark, or capture code.
4. A release or deployment change that alters the measured environment.

Re-run `OBL-VER-001` and `OBL-EVID-001` after any of these, and record the new
revision. A green result from an earlier tree is not current evidence.
