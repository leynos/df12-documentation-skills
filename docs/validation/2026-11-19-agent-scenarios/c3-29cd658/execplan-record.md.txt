# ExecPlan revision record – LIB rename, current milestone

This record states the change the existing, already-executing ExecPlan requires.
It does not replace the plan and must be applied to it. The plan remains the
self-contained execution contract; this record supplies the edits, the new
status, and the reasoning behind them.

Provisional identifiers follow the terms of reference: `LIB` is the published
0.8 package; `OLD` and `NEW` are the current and replacement public names;
`CONSUMER` is the single downstream project; `RFC-A` is the inherited RFC that
demands an alias. The plan already exists, so its file path, milestone
identifier, and decision numbering are not reproduced or invented here. Use the
plan's own current milestone identifier and its next free decision number.

## Status to record

Status: BLOCKED (inherited RFC alias demand conflicts with the pre-1.0
compatibility prohibition; awaiting explicit upstream resolution, TOR-OPEN-001)

Branch: unchanged.

Roadmap item: unchanged.

`BLOCKED` is required, not optional. The execplans contract says that when
evidence shows the approved design cannot or should not be followed, the plan
must not silently amend the implementation or plan around it. The plan records a
proposed deviation in `Decision log` with the affected identifiers, impacts,
options, and the upstream documents that must change, sets the status to
`BLOCKED`, and waits for explicit acceptance of the deviation before continuing.
That is precisely this situation: the executing rename reaches a live
compatibility demand that the governing policy prohibits.

Two starting states are possible and both end here. If the plan's milestone
text already repeats the `RFC-A` alias, that text is the deviation and must not
be implemented. If the plan never repeated it, the plan has still discovered an
inherited commitment that it may not leave unrecorded or unaccepted: an
unaccepted deviation and an unreconciled upstream conflict both prevent
`COMPLETE`.

`BLOCKED` here means "a governance decision is missing". It does not mean the
design is unclear. The technical design is complete enough to execute; the
prohibition and the permitted path are decided. Implementation stops at the
milestone boundary, not in the middle of an unvalidated edit.

## Why this status, in the plan's terms

The package is published at 0.8. The affected surface is therefore a pre-1.0
public API. The policy forbids source-API compatibility machinery for pre-1.0
APIs, and it states that a named consumer does not create an exception. `RFC-A`
demands an alias, so the demand falls inside the prohibited category. The RFC's
age gives it no precedence over the governing policy, and adopting the alias
would be compatibility theatre: an alias, deprecated entrypoint, or shim added
to preserve an obsolete source interface.

An executing plan may not work around a tolerance or a constraint. It also may
not rewrite the RFC silently, and it may not pretend the conflict does not
exist. The only compliant path is an atomic rename with a coordinated
migration, and the only way to resume is an explicit, dated upstream resolution.

No option within the supplied policy authorizes the alias. Resumption therefore
means one of two recorded outcomes:

1. The decision authority supersedes `RFC-A` on this point. This is the
   expected outcome; it requires a dated written record naming `RFC-A` and the
   superseded clause.
2. The owner of the governing policy changes that policy. This is outside the
   plan's authority and still requires the change to be recorded in the
   authoritative upstream artefacts. An RFC owner alone cannot grant it.

Until one outcome is recorded, the plan stays `BLOCKED`.

## What this record explicitly does not do

It does not add an alias, deprecated entrypoint, facade, wrapper, dual
implementation, or temporary shim. It does not rewrite `RFC-A`. It does not
invent an approver, a decision date, a consumer response, or a passing check.
It does not mark the plan `COMPLETE`, and it does not authorize resumption
before the conflict is accepted.

It also does not convert the RFC's demand into "remaining work". A plan that
lists the alias as a follow-up item has preserved the demand under a new name
and has not resolved it.
