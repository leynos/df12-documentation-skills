## Purpose / big picture

Implementation reports an architectural change that is claimed to be approved
but is absent from the technical design and the ADRs. Separately, a discovery
agent recommends additional hardening. Today a reader of the design cannot tell
which statements the decision owner accepted, and an implementer cannot tell
whether the hardening proposal is in scope.

After this work, the design and the ADR set will state the approved
architecture, the plan's conformance basis will name real identifiers and
revisions, and the hardening proposal will have an explicit disposition. Until
an approving authority acts, the observable state is: the plan is `BLOCKED`,
the design is unchanged, no hardening code is written, and no completion is
claimed.

## Constraints

- The design, ADRs, terms of reference, and roadmap are not edited from this
  session's authority. Changes to them are proposals.
- No compatibility shim, alias, facade, deprecated entrypoint, or dual

## Verification plan

### Abstract model

The reconciliation is a documentary state machine over four states: reported,
recorded, accepted, verified. Each affected upstream item starts at `reported`
and reaches `accepted` only through the decision owner. Implementation and
approval are separate axes; an item can be implemented and recorded yet remain
unaccepted.

### Invariants and lemmas

- `INV-LINKS`: every statement the approved change invalidates is either
  updated or explicitly marked unaffected in the design revision.
- `INV-AUTHORITY`: no record attributes acceptance to an actor or date absent
  from the approval record.
- `INV-EVIDENCE`: every affected verification obligation is re-discharged or
  recorded as unverified with the gap and owner named.
- `L-COMPLETE`: `INCOMPLETE` cannot be reached while any affected item remains
  at `reported` or `recorded`. This follows from `INV-LINKS` and
  `INV-AUTHORITY` applied to the completion gate.

The system-level invariants introduced by the architectural change itself are
`[OPEN]`: they cannot be stated without the approved decision's text. That
absence is a blocking gap, not an omitted section.

### Axioms (trusted, not verified here)

- `AXIOM-APPROVAL`: the approval record, once produced, is authentic and was
  granted within the signer's authority. Boundary: the decision owner; this
  plan verifies transcription, not authenticity.
- `AXIOM-REVISION`: the revisions supplied to the reconciler are those in
  force. Boundary: the repository's revision identifiers; verify hashes or
  commit IDs before use.
- `AXIOM-DESIGN`: the design revision was internally consistent before the
  change. Boundary: the design owner; this plan does not re-audit unrelated
  sections.

### Methods per obligation

- Obligation: `INV-LINKS` over the affected statement set.
  Method: trace audit by targeted search, plus reviewer diff.
  Rationale: the statement set is finite and documentary; exhaustive search is
  practical and proportionate.
  Domain: every design and ADR file in the supplied revision.
  Artefact: link table in the design change request.
  Evidence: before the edit, each targeted search returns the stale statement
  and the audit lists it; after the edit, the audit returns no stale statement
  and the table records each disposition.
  Non-vacuity: the before-audit must return at least one real stale statement.
  A negative control seeds one additional stale reference and confirms the
  audit reports it.

- Obligation: `INV-AUTHORITY` over every acceptance claim.
  Method: documentary cross-check against the approval record.
  Rationale: approval is a decision fact, not a runtime property.
  Domain: every status, date, and owner field changed by the reconciliation.
  Artefact: the ADR candidate's evidence list.
  Evidence: a field with no matching record fails the check; every field
  matches exactly one record on success.
  Non-vacuity: include an intentionally unsupported owner field in the
  check set and confirm it is rejected.

- Obligation: `INV-EVIDENCE` over the affected obligation inventory.
  Method: obligation-by-obligation diff, with each item marked
  re-discharged, deferred with acceptance, or unverified.
  Rationale: the change invalidates evidence scope, and only a per-obligation
  review can show which evidence survives.
  Domain: every verification obligation linked to the changed requirements.
  Artefact: an updated obligation table in the design revision.
  Evidence: no obligation retains a passing result from a pre-change revision
  without an explicit re-run or an explicit invalidation note.
  Non-vacuity: at least one obligation must be genuinely invalidated; an
  inventory where everything survives unchanged is treated as a failed audit.

### Formal verification decision

No model checking or formal proof is proposed for the reconciliation itself.
The obligations are finite documentary properties, and the methods above
discharge them. The architectural change may require its own formal or
property-based obligations once its text is known; that decision belongs to the
revisited design and must not be assumed here.
