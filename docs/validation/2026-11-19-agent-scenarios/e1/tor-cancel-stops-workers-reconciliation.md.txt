# Supervisor cancellation promise – reconciliation record

This record applies the terms-of-reference evidence rules to a promise that an
integration probe contradicts. It is a **proposed reconciliation**, not an
accepted revision of the Terms of Reference (ToR) and not evidence that anyone
has approved a change. The approved promise remains in force until its owner
says otherwise.

Status: proposed; upstream revision not accepted.

Audience: the ToR owner, the Architecture Decision Record (ADR) owner, the
technical design owner, and the engineer executing the related ExecPlan.

Companion documents: the ToR, the ADR, and
`execplan-cancel-supervisor-stops-workers.md` in this directory. Exact paths
and revisions are `[OPEN]`; the scenario did not supply them and they were not
readable under this task's isolation rule.

Date and version: 2026-11-19, draft 1.

Authority and evidence basis: no approval or approval evidence was supplied.
The promise is recorded as accepted intent because the scenario presents it as
what the ToR and ADR promise. The probe is recorded as an observed baseline
because the scenario presents it as an integration result. Neither claim was
independently verified here.

## 1. The promise

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| ID | `LOCAL-TOR-PROMISE-CANCEL-STOPS-WORKERS` (local handle; upstream ID `[OPEN]`) |
| Statement | Cancelling a supervisor stops its workers. |
| Kind | Accepted requirement and product intent. |
| Source | ToR, and the same promise in an ADR. Paths and revisions `[OPEN]`. |
| Evidence label | `[KNOWN]` as a statement of intent; it is not an empirical claim about the current build. |
| Approval state | Stated by the scenario as promised. Approval record and decision authority `[OPEN]`. |
| Downstream links | `LOCAL-ADR-DECISION-CANCEL-STOPS-WORKERS`, `LOCAL-TDD-REQ-CANCEL-STOPS-WORKERS`, `GAP-WORKERS-SURVIVE-SUPERVISOR-EXIT`. |
| Failure consequence if unmet | Operators cannot reclaim worker capacity or trust cancellation; orphaned workers keep running after the supervisor is gone. |
| Expiry or review trigger | The probe result recorded below. |

The promise is a universal lifecycle claim: for every worker the supervisor
started, the worker is not alive after the supervisor exits. It is not a claim
about the cancellation signal alone, and it is not satisfied by the supervisor
exiting.

The statement does not define:

- the observation time after exit at which liveness is checked;
- whether a grace period is allowed;
- whether workers that existed before the supervisor started are covered;
- what counts as alive (running, sleeping, zombie, orphan);
- whether every worker starting mode is included.

These are gaps in the requirement, not concessions to be inferred from the
implementation.

| Kind | Observed baseline behaviour of the current implementation. |

| Evidence label | `[KNOWN]` within the scope the probe exercises; wider scope `[OPEN]`. |
| Scope stated | Supervisor cancelled; supervisor exits; at least one worker remains alive. |
| Scope limits | Exact command, output, worker count, PIDs, cancellation action, observation window, and liveness check were not supplied. |
| What it does not establish | That the promise is unwanted, infeasible, or approved for revision. It establishes only that this run contradicted the promise under the conditions exercised. |

The probe is a candidate counterexample to a universal claim. One genuine
counterexample is sufficient to falsify the promise as written. Its force
therefore depends on two conditions: that the observation is accurate
(`AXIOM-PROBE-OBSERVATION`), and that the observation time and worker set fall
under the promise (`AXIOM-PROBE-GOVERNS-PROMISE`). The second condition is
currently unverified because the requirement is silent on timing and worker
scope. Until it is settled, the correct label for the overall situation is
"approved intent contradicted by observed baseline, with measurement
ambiguity", not "requirement disproved".

## 3. Why the promise is not rewritten

Documented intent and observed behaviour are different evidence classes. The
ToR rules require both to be recorded, and forbid replacing an approved outcome
with whatever the implementation happens to achieve. A probe that shows live
workers does not by itself decide whether:

1. the implementation is defective and the promise stands; or
2. the promise is wrong, infeasible, or over-broad and should change.

Option 1 keeps the promise and changes the code. Option 2 changes the product
contract and requires the ToR owner plus the ADR owner, with downstream design
and roadmap reconciliation. Choosing option 2 because the probe exists would
let the implementation approve its own requirement change. That is exactly the
failure this record prevents.

The contradiction is therefore recorded as a gap, and the related ExecPlan is
`BLOCKED` pending an explicit choice.

## 4. Proposed resolutions (none accepted)

- **O1 – honour the promise.** Keep the ToR and ADR as written; change the
  implementation so that no worker started by the supervisor remains alive
  when it exits. Upstream ToR change: none. ADR change: none, unless the
  cancellation mechanism must change. Design change: failure-mode and
  lifecycle detail, if the existing design cannot deliver it. Risk: the fix
  may be larger than expected for workers that ignore the first termination
  request.
- **O2 – amend the promise.** Replace it with an explicit weaker guarantee,
  such as "cancellation requests that workers stop; the supervisor may exit
  before they do." Upstream ToR and ADR revision required. Design and roadmap
  acceptance evidence must follow. This deliberately removes a safety
  guarantee and needs a stated rationale from the owner; it is not an
  implementation detail.
- **O3 – qualify the promise.** Keep it for a defined grace period, a defined
  worker set, or cooperative workers only. Upstream ToR and ADR revision
  required, plus a precise measurement. This is the likely outcome if the
  documents intended a bounded grace period but never said so.

Recommendation: settle the operational definition first. If the documents
intended unconditional termination, choose O1. If they intended a bounded or
cooperative guarantee, choose O3 and state the bound explicitly. O2 should be
chosen only with an explicit decision to stop promising termination, not as a
convenience.

## 5. Open questions

Each question gates work; none is resolved by this record.

- **OPEN-1. What does "stops" require?** Precise liveness statement: no
  worker alive at any time after exit, or no worker alive after a grace period
  `G`, or termination requested but not confirmed? Gates the regression test
  and the design requirement. Resolution: the ToR owner states the intended
  guarantee. Owner: `[OPEN]`.
- **OPEN-2. Is the probe a valid counterexample under that definition?** Gates
  whether the promise is actually falsified. Resolution: reproduce the probe
  and record the observation window, worker census, cancellation action, and
  liveness check. Owner: the implementing engineer, once a repository is
  available.
- **OPEN-3. Which workers are covered?** Direct children only, or the whole
  process group including grandchildren and daemonized workers? Gates the
  implementation and its boundary cases. Resolution: the design or ADR states
  the worker set, or the owner classifies it. Owner: `[OPEN]`.
- **OPEN-4. Who may accept a change?** The scenario names no authority. Gates
  O1, O2, and O3. Resolution: name the ToR owner and ADR owner, or their
  delegate, and record their decision. Owner: `[OPEN]`.
- **OPEN-5. Do the ToR and ADR state the same thing?** The scenario says both
  promise termination, but not in identical words. Gates which document to
  revise first. Resolution: compare the exact texts and record any difference.
  Owner: `[OPEN]`.
- **OPEN-6. What design and roadmap links depend on the promise?** Gates the
  impact assessment and the completion condition. Resolution: name the design
  revision, roadmap acceptance criterion, and release or deployment claim that
  cite it. Owner: `[OPEN]`.

## 6. Downstream impact

- **Technical design.** If one exists, its lifecycle and failure-mode sections
  now have a gap: `GAP-WORKERS-SURVIVE-SUPERVISOR-EXIT`. The design must either
  record the mechanism that enforces the promise or record the accepted weaker
  guarantee. It must not silently inherit a passing test as proof.
- **ADR.** If O1 is accepted, the decision stands and the implementation is
  corrected; add an implementation note only if useful. If O2 or O3 is
  accepted, amend or supersede the ADR with the new decision, rationale, and
  consequences, and preserve the old decision as history.
- **Roadmap.** Any acceptance criterion that assumes cancellation reclaims
  workers is currently unproven. Do not mark it satisfied. Update it to match
  the accepted resolution.
- **ExecPlan.** The plan is `BLOCKED` at D1. Its milestones, verification
  obligations, and trace links depend on the answer to OPEN-1 and OPEN-4.
- **Release and deployment.** No claim is made here about any release. A
  merged change would not by itself establish the promise, and this record does
  not establish that any release occurred.

## 7. Handoff and feedback route

Readiness: blocked for completion and ready only for bounded discovery
(reproduce the probe and settle OPEN-1). The ToR is not superseded, and no
revision is accepted.

Feedback route if implementation later falsifies an assumption: return to
this record, update the probe entry with the new evidence and its revision,
reassess the design and roadmap impacts, and record the proposed revision here
before any upstream edit. The decision authority for a promise change is the
ToR owner together with the ADR owner; the implementing agent may recommend but
may not accept.
