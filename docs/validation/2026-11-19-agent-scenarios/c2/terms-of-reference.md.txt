## 1. Background and motivation

A stable repository exposes a helper that only its tests call. The helper's
shape no longer serves the code, and maintaining it in its current form costs
more than reshaping it. The suggested route is to keep the old tests compiling
through a compatibility shim.

The suggestion is worth naming precisely because it is attractive: it makes the
test suite appear untouched. The hidden cost is an interface that exists only
for tests, retained after its purpose has gone. This document settles what the
work is for, what it will not do, and how the tests' real value survives the
change.

## 2. Domain and market context

This section does not apply in the usual form. The work is an internal
maintenance change to a library's test support, with no market, competitor, or
external field of practice at stake. Prior art is limited to the repository's
own test suite and the generic refactoring conventions below. Recording this
explicitly prevents padding the document with invented landscape.

The one domain convention that does apply is the distinction between an
interface's *shape* and the *behaviour* its tests assert. A test is evidence
about behaviour. Its call text is not evidence about anything a consumer needs.

## 3. Users and stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Concern | Question the design must answer | Evidence needed | Decision right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maintainer (primary) | Change the helper's shape without inheriting a permanent compatibility burden. | Does the reshape remove the old surface while keeping the tests that mattered? | Passing suite, absence search, test disposition record | Approves or rejects the change |
| Reviewer (secondary) | No accidental public surface or hidden production coupling appears. | Is the helper's visibility the minimum the current test locations require? | Visibility audit against the tree and release tags | May block the change |
| Continuous integration (actor) | Every committed state builds and passes. | Does each commit compile with no mixed old and new interfaces? | Gate transcripts | None |

There are no external users. That absence is the reason the compatibility
question has the answer it does, so it belongs in the record rather than being
left implicit.

## 4. Job to be done

> When a maintainer needs to change how an internal test-only helper works,
> they want to reshape the helper and its test callers in one movement, so they
> can stop carrying an interface that exists only to keep old test text
> compiling.

The job's outcome is a coherent internal surface, not preserving a particular
signature. A reader who expects the opposite should read §6.2 before designing.

## 5. Scope

### 5.1 Goals

`TOR-GOAL-001` – Reshape the helper into one coherent interface without
changing the observable behaviour its tests assert.

`TOR-GOAL-002` – Retain the behavioural coverage the existing tests provide.
Coverage means the assertions, not the source text of the tests.

### 5.2 Non-goals

`TOR-NONGOAL-001` – No compatibility shim, alias, `deprecated` forwarding
entrypoint, dual implementation, facade, or temporary wrapper for the old
helper, including one introduced only for the duration of the change. A caller
needing the old shape is a caller that has not yet been migrated.

`TOR-NONGOAL-002` – No change to production behaviour, no new public API, no
new dependency, and no feature flag unless a test location requires one to
compile at all.

`TOR-NONGOAL-003` – No rewrite of unrelated tests, modules, or fixtures beyond
what the migration needs.

## 6. Success criteria

`TOR-SUCCESS-001` – The tree builds and the suite passes with the old surface
deleted, not merely marked deprecated.

`TOR-SUCCESS-002` – Every pre-change test has a recorded disposition: migrated,
rewritten to assert behaviour rather than shape, or retired with a stated
reason. No test disappears silently, and no assertion is dropped because it
failed to compile.

`TOR-SUCCESS-003` – A search of the source tree finds no reference to the old
symbol. The build is the primary check; the search is corroboration.

`TOR-SUCCESS-004` – The helper's visibility is no wider than the current test
locations require, and a reviewer can see why the chosen visibility is the
minimum.

User-facing and strategic success measures do not apply; this change produces
no product-visible outcome. Operational success is §6.1 and §6.2. Recording the
inapplicable categories avoids inventing adoption metrics for a refactor.

## 7. Constraints and assumptions

### 7.1 Hard constraints

`TOR-CONSTRAINT-001` – The helper is a test-only support surface. Source-API
compatibility machinery is prohibited for that category. The prohibition is
independent of repository maturity and is not waived by naming a consumer.

`TOR-CONSTRAINT-002` – All callers change in the same coherent change. On the
current evidence the callers are tests only; §7.3 records the possibility that
this is false.

`TOR-CONSTRAINT-003` – The change must not widen the helper's surface to make
migration easier.

### 7.2 Conflicts and inherited commitments

`CR-1` – The brief suggests a compatibility shim. It conflicts with
`TOR-CONSTRAINT-001`. Disposition: the suggestion is not adopted, and the
conflict is recorded here rather than resolved silently. Preserving the tests'
intent satisfies the brief's evident purpose. Preserving their old call shape
does not.

### 7.3 Assumptions

`TOR-ASSUMPTION-001` – No consumer outside this repository can reach the helper
in a released artefact.

Failure consequence: if false, the surface is not test-only as described, the
classification and policy assessment must be redone, and the work stops.
Verification route: visibility audit of the tree and release tags before any
edit. Expiry: any visibility change, new release, or rebase.

`TOR-ASSUMPTION-002` – The existing tests exercise the helper's observable
behaviour closely enough that behaviour preservation is observable.

Failure consequence: if false, the reshape may change behaviour without any
test noticing. Verification route: a seeded behavioural fault that the migrated
suite must reject. Expiry: when the suite's coverage is materially reduced or
replaced.

### 7.4 Dependencies

None external. The only tooling dependency is the repository's own build and
test toolchain, which the execution plan must name from the tree rather than
assume.

## 8. Open questions

`TOR-OPEN-001` – Is any part of the helper's current surface reachable by a
consumer outside this repository in a released artefact?

Why it matters: it gates the classification that justifies rejecting the shim.
Resolution criteria: a visibility audit of the module, the crate or package
manifest, and the latest formal release tag, showing whether the symbol is
exported and consumed. Owner: maintainer. Resolution path: the execution plan's
inventory milestone. Suggested default if no consumer is found: proceed, and
record the audit as evidence.

`TOR-OPEN-002` – Which existing tests assert incidental implementation detail
rather than behaviour, and therefore need rewriting or retirement rather than a
mechanical migration?

Why it matters: it decides individual test dispositions and protects against
coverage loss disguised as migration. Resolution criteria: each test read and
classified. Owner: implementing agent. Resolution path: the inventory and
reconciliation milestones.

A terms of reference with no open questions would be a claim of certainty this
document cannot support.

## Handoff readiness

Ready for design on the problem statement and scope. Not approved for
implementation. The terms of reference does not authorise the design of the new
interface, the sequencing of the change, or its acceptance evidence; those
belong to `tech-design.md` and `execplan.md`. `TOR-OPEN-001` is a design gate,
not a reason to delay design work: the design can proceed on the stated
assumption and must treat a contrary finding as a blocking deviation.

Companion artefacts: three domain terms are candidates for `docs/context.md` if
it exists – *test-only surface*, *disposition*, and *compatibility shim*. No ADR
candidate arises: the compatibility decision follows existing policy rather
than making a new, hard-to-reverse choice. There is no roadmap implication; the
change has one coherent slice and no sequencing decision.

Feedback route: if implementation falsifies `TOR-ASSUMPTION-001` or
`TOR-ASSUMPTION-002`, reopen §7 here first, then impact-check `tech-design.md`
and `execplan.md` before claiming the change complete. A proposed revision is
recorded as a proposal until the maintainer accepts it.
