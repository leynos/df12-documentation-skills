# New library (working title) – terms of reference

Status: draft v0.1; not reviewed, not accepted.

Audience: the sponsor or author of the brief; the developers who will design,
implement, and maintain the library; whoever writes the technical design and
the roadmap.

Companion documents: the brief (path and revision not recorded; its text was
not available to the session that produced this draft). No `docs/context.md`,
technical design, roadmap, or ADR exists yet. See "Handoff and readiness" for
what each downstream artefact may inherit.

Date and version: 2026-11-21, v0.1 (first draft).

Authority and evidence basis: no approver is identified. The only evidence
available is a supplied statement characterizing the brief:

> A small new library has a clear brief, no legacy implementation, and no
> multi-actor workflow or imposed technology choice.

No repository artefact was inspected. Last verification: 2026-11-21, against
that statement alone. A statement inside the brief is the sponsor's request,
not an established fact, until the brief is ingested and any empirical claim in
it is separately supported.

Evidence labels: `[KNOWN]` is established by the cited source within its stated
scope; `[ASSUMED]` is a reasonable inference that needs confirmation; `[OPEN]`
is unresolved.

## 1. Background and motivation

[KNOWN] The product is a library: reusable code that other code calls, rather

[OPEN] The field of practice, its established conventions, the prior art the

## 3. Market context

[OPEN] No direct alternative, adjacent tool, or current default is named. The

Gates: TOR-OPEN-002 and TOR-OPEN-003.

[OPEN] Whether the gap is one of capability, ergonomics, cost, or trust. Gates:

## 4. Users and stakeholders

### Primary user (caller)

[ASSUMED] The primary user is a developer who calls the library from their own
code. Source: a structural inference from "library". Confirmation is needed,
because a library can also be built for an internal platform team, a plugin
author, or an end-user scripting audience, and each choice implies a different
interface and support posture.

[OPEN] Their ecosystem, role, seniority, technical fluency, the situation that
triggers use, what they care about, and what they will ignore or dislike.
Gates: TOR-OPEN-002.

### Secondary users and non-users

[OPEN] Whether anyone consumes the library indirectly, for example reviewers of
code that embeds it, and which developers the library is explicitly not for.
Naming non-users is the usual way to bound a primary-user definition, so this
is a material gap.

### Stakeholders

Candidate roles only. No person, owner, or decision right is established.

| Candidate role | Material concern | Design question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief sponsor | Capability at justifiable cost | What outcome justifies the work, and what would falsify it? |
| Maintaining developer | Correct, supportable surface | What compatibility obligations attach to the API? |

Evidence each role would need: the sponsor, a demonstrated outcome against the
stated goal; the maintainer, an interface the design can commit to supporting.
Neither is available yet.

Whether these roles are held by one person, and who may accept scope changes,
is `[OPEN]` (TOR-OPEN-007). Decision rights are not inferred from role titles.

## 6. Scope

### 6.1 Goals

[OPEN] No goal is recorded. A goal must be a falsifiable outcome, and the

### 6.2 Non-goals

[OPEN] No non-goal is recorded. A defensible non-goal is an exclusion a

Two boundaries are fixed by the supplied statement: the work is greenfield

## 7. Success criteria

[OPEN] No user-facing, operational, or strategic signal is recorded, and no

The dimensions to elicit, in the sponsor's terms, are:

- User-facing: whether callers adopt the library and complete the job it

- Operational: the maintenance and support burden the library imposes.
- Strategic: the capability the sponsor gains from its existence.

Gates: TOR-OPEN-005.

## 8. Constraints and assumptions

### 8.1 Hard constraints

TOR-CONSTRAINT-001 [KNOWN] Greenfield: no legacy implementation. Source: the
supplied statement. Consequence: no existing behaviour, migration, or
backward-compatibility obligation is inherited, and this document records
none. Whether a pre-release surface or an early caller exists is not
established. Source-API compatibility machinery for a pre-1.0 library is
excluded by the governing policy in any case; an inherited demand for it would
be a conflict to resolve explicitly, not a reason for an alias or shim.

TOR-CONSTRAINT-002 [KNOWN] No technology choice is imposed. Source: the
supplied statement. Consequence: language, runtime, packaging, distribution,
and dependency selection are design decisions. This document records an
environmental constraint only if one is supplied, for example a mandated
runtime or distribution channel, and states no preference of its own.

TOR-CONSTRAINT-003 [KNOWN] No multi-actor workflow is described. Source: the
supplied statement. Consequence: actor orchestration and inter-actor ordering
are outside the recorded scope. A later change to this fact invalidates the
scenario-probe omission in §5 and widens the design's verification scope.

### 8.2 Assumptions

TOR-ASSUMPTION-001 [ASSUMED] The supplied statement accurately characterizes
the brief. Failure consequence: if any of the four stated properties is wrong,
§3–§8 change and the design's scope changes with them; an unreported mandated
platform would invalidate the technology options the design may consider.
Verification: ingest the brief and confirm with the sponsor. Expiry: on brief
ingestion.

TOR-ASSUMPTION-002 [ASSUMED] The brief is the authoritative statement of
intent. Failure consequence: a charter, ticket, or prior discussion may carry
different goals or constraints, and the design would build against the wrong
one. Verification: the sponsor confirms authority and identifies any competing
artefact. Expiry: when a second authoritative artefact appears.

### 8.3 Dependencies

[OPEN] No external dependency is recorded. Whether the library depends on a
runtime, a distribution channel, a licence, or an upstream project is not
established. None is inferred. Gates: TOR-OPEN-006.

## 9. Open questions

Owner: not identified for any item. That fact is recorded rather than guessed;
TOR-OPEN-007 resolves the authority question.

**TOR-OPEN-001 – What is the brief's exact content and revision?** Gates every
section. Resolution: the brief's text is supplied and quoted, and its path and
revision are recorded. Path: ingest the brief before any elicitation question
is asked.

**TOR-OPEN-002 – Who is the primary caller, and what situation triggers use?**
Gates §3, §4, §5, §7, and the design's interface section. Resolution: a named
developer role, ecosystem, and triggering situation that the sponsor confirms.
Path: elicitation after TOR-OPEN-001.

**TOR-OPEN-003 – What capability must the library provide, and what does
"small" mean for it?** Gates §3, §6.1, and the design's requirement set.
Resolution: at least one falsifiable outcome, plus the intended surface size,
expressed as callable operations, dependency count, or an explicit statement
that size is not a constraint. Path: derive from the brief and confirm with the
sponsor.

**TOR-OPEN-004 – Which exclusions would a reasonable reader assume are in
scope but are not?** Gates §6.2 and the design's alternatives. Resolution: a
short list of plausible exclusions, each with a redirect where one exists.
Path: elicitation after TOR-OPEN-003.

**TOR-OPEN-005 – What signals success, against which baseline and target?**
Gates §7. Resolution: for each material measure, a signal, measurement
condition, evidence source, target or an explicit finding that it is not
currently measurable, and an owner. Path: elicitation, then user or market
research where the sponsor cannot answer.

**TOR-OPEN-006 – Which environmental constraints must the design obey?** Gates
the design's technology selection: target runtime, distribution channel,
licence, support lifetime, and any consuming organization's mandate.
Resolution: each constraint stated with provenance, or an explicit statement
that none applies. Path: brief ingestion and sponsor confirmation.

**TOR-OPEN-007 – Who may approve this document and accept scope changes?**
Gates handoff, approval state, and the design's authority to proceed.
Resolution: a named role or person, and the approval evidence that will record
acceptance. Path: sponsor confirmation.

## Handoff and readiness

Readiness: bounded discovery only. The problem space is not established, so the
scope is not ready for a technical design. Resolving TOR-OPEN-001 to
TOR-OPEN-006 is the bounded work; it needs the brief and a short elicitation,
not a discovery campaign.

Approval state: v0.1 draft, unreviewed, with no identified approver. Nothing in
this document authorizes implementation.

Identifiers carried forward: TOR-CONSTRAINT-001 to TOR-CONSTRAINT-003,
TOR-ASSUMPTION-001 and TOR-ASSUMPTION-002, and TOR-OPEN-001 to TOR-OPEN-007. No
goal, non-goal, or success-criterion identifier is issued while the
corresponding content is unresolved. A later issue must not recycle an
identifier used here.

Downstream:

- `tech-design-doc`: may begin only after TOR-OPEN-002 to TOR-OPEN-006 are
  resolved or explicitly waived by the decision authority. Its conformance
  basis should cite this revision. Technology selection is its decision, not
  this document's.
- `roadmap-doc`: not started. It needs goals and success criteria that do not
  yet exist.
- `execplans`: not started. It requires an approved design slice and its own
  approval gate. The installed skill and template were inspected on
  2026-11-21; the installed files carry no version identifier.
- `docs/context.md`: no domain term has been established, so no entry is
  proposed. The first terms from the brief are candidates.
- ADRs: none surfaced. Technology selection and API-stability policy may
  warrant one during design; that decision belongs to the design.

Feedback route: if the brief contradicts a `[KNOWN]` item or supplies a
constraint absent from §8, revise the affected sections, re-check the design
outline, and record the change as proposed until the decision authority accepts
it. Approval of this document is separate from any implementation, verification,
release, or deployment state.
