## Established and absent facts

The supplied statement establishes, within its own scope:

- F1. The subject is a software library, not an application, service, or
  platform.
- F2. An explicit brief exists, and the supplied statement describes it as
  clear.
- F3. There is no legacy implementation.
- F4. The brief describes no multi-actor workflow.
- F5. No technology choice is imposed on the design.

None of these establishes the library's domain, purpose, callers, competing
alternatives, goals, non-goals, success measures, environmental constraints, or
decision authority. F2 establishes what the sponsor requests; it does not
establish the truth of any empirical claim inside the brief.

## Tailoring decisions

1. Scenario probe omitted. The optional probe exists for multiple actors,
   lifecycle behaviour, integration paths, and exceptional flows. F4 removes
   that trigger, and no other trigger is visible, so the probe would test
   nothing at v0.1. Revisit it if the brief introduces concurrency, background
   work, or an external integration.
2. No baseline, gap register, or migration programme. The architecture
   contract directs greenfield work to state that no implementation baseline
   exists and to avoid manufacturing an empty legacy architecture.
3. No technology constraint recorded. An imposed platform would be a sourced
   constraint; with none imposed, technology selection belongs to
   `tech-design-doc`. The terms of reference must not smuggle a choice.
4. Minimal records. A small library warrants a few identifiers, two stakeholder
   concerns, and a short handoff. No goal, success, or assumption identifier is
   issued for content that does not yet exist.
5. No invented content. Goals, non-goals, competitors, owners, approvers,
   thresholds, and dependencies are marked `[OPEN]`, not filled with
   plausible-sounding generalities.

## Elicitation order

One action or question per turn, in dependency order:

1. Ingest the brief; quote its content and record its path and revision.
2. TOR-OPEN-001 – confirm which artefact is authoritative and current.
3. TOR-OPEN-002 – identify the primary caller and the triggering situation.
4. TOR-OPEN-003 – establish the required capability as verifiable goals, and
   define what "small" means for the surface.
5. TOR-OPEN-004 – name exclusions a reasonable reader might assume are in
   scope.
6. TOR-OPEN-005 – establish success signals and any baselines or targets.
7. TOR-OPEN-006 – establish environmental constraints: runtime, distribution
   channel, licence, and support lifetime.
8. TOR-OPEN-007 – establish scope-change and approval authority.
