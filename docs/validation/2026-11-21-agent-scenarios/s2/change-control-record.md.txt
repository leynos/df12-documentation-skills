## 1. Situation

Implementation is proceeding under a technical design and an ExecPlan whose
`Conformance basis` cites that design. Two events occur:

1. A discovery agent recommends extra hardening.
2. Implementation finds an approved architectural change that the technical
   design and ADRs do not record.

Neither event can be absorbed silently. The first is advice, not authority. The
second is an approved change whose upstream record is missing, so the executing
plan no longer conforms to its declared basis. The two items are treated as
independent unless evidence links them.

| B. Architectural change | Implementation | Approved | Approval evidence absent; design and ADR absence reported, not inspected | Unknown | None attached | Blocking reconciliation |

A discovery agent may recommend a change without approving it or completing the
parent goal. A reviewer suggestion or subordinate agent report is input, not an
approval or a licence to complete a parent goal. The label "approved" in the
implementation report is likewise a claim: working code, or a merged change,
establishes what landed, not that a decision was authorized.

## 3. Item A – hardening recommendation

### 3.1 Status

`PROPOSED / ADVISORY`. Not approved. Not in the terms of reference, the design,
or the plan scope. No implementation is authorized on its basis.

### 3.2 Why it is not accepted here

"Extra hardening" does not identify an asset, threat, failure mode, control,
cost, or owner. Accepting it would expand scope on the strength of an advisory
voice. Rejecting it silently would discard potentially material risk
information. The record therefore preserves it as an open proposal.

### 3.3 Evidence required before a decision

Ask in dependency order, one question at a time:

1. Which asset, trust boundary, or failure mode is under-protected, and what
   evidence shows the gap?
2. Which approved ToR goal, constraint, success criterion, or design
   requirement does the gap threaten?
3. What control is proposed, and what alternatives exist?
4. What are the cost, operational, performance, compatibility, and verification
   impacts?
5. Who owns the residual risk, and who has authority to accept or reject the
   change?

### 3.4 Classification pending

- ToR-level: it changes a hard constraint, risk appetite, success criterion, or
  scope boundary.
- Design-level: it adds or changes a control, component, interface, trust
  boundary, or verification obligation.
- Implementation-level: an approved requirement already covers it and the work
  stays within ExecPlan tolerances. Even then, record the choice.
- Out of scope: record the rationale and redirect.

### 3.5 Disposition and gating

Record-local ID: `OPEN-HARDEN-01`. No upstream identifier is assigned, and no
existing identifier is reused.

Resolution criteria: a named decision authority records accept, defer, or
reject with rationale and affected upstream links.

Gating: the recommendation does not by itself block completion of the current
work. It blocks if the authority accepts it into scope, or if it demonstrates
that an approved requirement or acceptance criterion is unmet. Either outcome
requires a design or plan revision before completion.

## 4. Item B – approved architectural change not in design or ADR

### 4.1 Status

`APPROVED (claimed) / IMPLEMENTED (claimed or in progress) / UNRECORDED
upstream / BLOCKING`.

The change may be legitimately approved. The reported defect is that the
technical design and ADRs, which are the authoritative record of architecture,
do not contain it. The ExecPlan's `Conformance basis` therefore cites a design
that no longer describes the reported approved architecture. The design and ADR
record was not inspected in this workspace, so the absence is a report to
verify, not an observed fact.

### 4.2 Evidence required

- Approval record: decision authority, date, scope, and revision.
- Change description: affected components, interfaces, trust boundaries, data
  formats, dependencies, and failure modes.
- Implementation evidence: what actually landed, at which revision.
- Confirmation that the approving authority is the design authority, and that
  the change remains within approved ToR intent.

If any of these is absent, the change is a proposal pending evidence, not an
approved change.

Evidence expiry: re-check the approval record and design revision after any
rebase, upstream merge, or design or ADR amendment. Implementation and
verification evidence remains tied to the revision that produced it; an earlier
green result does not become current evidence after the architecture changes.

### 4.3 Bidirectional impact analysis

| Upstream or downstream artefact | Impact to assess | Required action if affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ToR goals, constraints, success criteria, assumptions | Does the change contradict or falsify an assumption, constraint, or criterion? | Record evidence and propose a ToR revision. Do not rewrite intent to match code. |
| Technical design | Missing decision, design element, contract, failure mode, or verification obligation | Amend the design with decision, alternatives, rationale, consequences, and trace links. |
| ADR | Hard-to-reverse decision absent from the decision record | Create or amend an ADR and link it from the design. |
| Roadmap | Sequencing, phase hypothesis, or outcome affected | Re-check the affected phase and record the change. |
| ExecPlan | Stale `Conformance basis`, milestone acceptance, and trace links | Update the basis and links; keep status `BLOCKED` until reconciliation is accepted. |
| Compatibility | Interface or persisted/wire-format obligation | Classify the surface. No compatibility machinery for private, test-only, pre-1.0, or unreleased APIs. Assess released 1.0+ contracts and deployed state separately. |
| Verification | Invariants, lemmas, axioms, and evidence invalidated by the architecture change | Reopen the obligations. Mark unexecuted checks planned, not passed. |

### 4.4 Required sequence

1. Attach approval evidence and confirm the approving authority.
2. Amend the technical design or ADR; see the amendment request.
3. Re-check every affected ToR, design, roadmap, and ExecPlan link in both
   directions.
4. Record explicit acceptance of the reconciled deviation in the ExecPlan
   `Decision log`.
5. Update `Conformance basis` to the amended revision.
6. Resume implementation and verification.

### 4.5 Prohibited resolutions

- Treating working code or a merged change as proof of approval.
- Editing the design to legitimize implementation without approval evidence.
- Continuing implementation or claiming completion while the design is stale.
- Inventing owners, identifiers, dates, or revisions.
- Adding aliases, facades, deprecated entrypoints, dual implementations, or
  wrappers to avoid recording the change.
- Silently converting the change into a proposal, or the proposal into an
  approved change.
