# Released 2.1 function change – terms of reference constraint revision

Status: Draft v0.1, not accepted.

Date: 2026-11-21.

Audience: maintainers, the release authority, and the technical-design author.

Companion documents: `00-intake-and-elicitation.md`,
`02-technical-design.md`, and `03-execplan.md` in this directory. The target
repository's own terms of reference, `context.md`, ADRs, roadmap, and release
notes were not inspected; the invoking session's isolation rule prohibits
reading them.

Authority and evidence basis: the supplied change statement. No approver,
approval date, or acceptance evidence was supplied. Document acceptance and
release state are separate: this revision does not approve the change and does
not establish that any implementation, verification, release, or deployment
has occurred.

## Purpose

This revision does not restate the package's terms of reference. It records the
problem-space constraints that the change inherits, so that the technical
design classifies the surface correctly instead of inferring compatibility from
the branch position. Per the tailoring rule in
`terms-of-reference-doc/references/problem-contract.md` §"Tailor the records,
not the obligations" (inspected 2026-11-21), only the sections that resolve a
real question appear below.

## Hard constraints

### TOR-CONSTRAINT-001 – compatibility commitment on the released surface

Statement: the function's published 2.1 contract is subject to an explicit
compatibility commitment.

Provenance: supplied change statement.

Evidence status: `[KNOWN]` existence. The commitment's exact text, duration,
notice period, permitted change classes, and removal conditions are `[OPEN]`
(TOR-OPEN-002).

Binding effect: the change must not break the committed surface in place. Any
in-place breaking change, removal, or semantic change is permitted only by the
commitment's own process, at the release it permits, with the notice it
requires.

Known downstream items: the technical design's compatibility assessment; the
ExecPlan's compatibility decision and removal gate.

### TOR-CONSTRAINT-002 – deployed external consumer

Statement: at least one external consumer is deployed against the released
function.

Provenance: supplied change statement.

Evidence status: `[KNOWN]` existence. The consumer's identity, deployed
version, deployment date, and usage profile are `[OPEN]` (TOR-OPEN-003).

Binding effect: the maintainers cannot assume that the consumer upgrades in
lockstep with the package. A migration must give the consumer a path that the
commitment supports.

Known downstream items: stakeholder table; characterization and consumer
acceptance obligations.

### TOR-CONSTRAINT-003 – release lineage fixes the obligation, not the branch tip

Statement: the compatibility obligation attaches to a surface that has appeared
in a formal release tag. The function's release in 2.1 is such a surface. The
current branch being ahead of the tag does not withdraw the published 2.1
contract; it means only that edits made on the branch are not yet released.

Provenance: supplied facts EV-001 and EV-004, interpreted under the df12 policy
in `terms-of-reference-doc/references/problem-contract.md` §"Compatibility is
an explicit constraint, never a milestone side effect" (inspected 2026-11-21):
"Evaluate the surface and its release lineage, not just whether the branch is
ahead of a tag."

Binding effect: the design must not apply the "unreleased API changes ahead of
the latest formal release tag" exemption to this function. The exemption may
apply to symbols that exist only on the branch and in no release tag; it does
not reach back to a shipped 2.1 declaration.

Known downstream items: the technical design's classification verdict and
rejected alternatives.

## Goals and non-goals

### TOR-GOAL-001 – deliver the change without breaking committed behaviour

Deliver the intended change while every guarantee of the published 2.1 contract
remains true for committed consumers.

### TOR-GOAL-002 – provide a migration path

Provide a documented path so the deployed consumer can adopt the new contract
on its own schedule within the commitment's terms.

### TOR-NONGOAL-001 – unrelated API redesign

This work does not redesign other public surfaces and does not add a
package-wide compatibility layer. Unrelated surfaces follow the package's
normal release policy.

### TOR-NONGOAL-002 – prescribing the mechanism

This revision does not choose between a wrapper, a renamed entry point, an
overload, a deprecation cycle, or a coordinated migration. That is a technical
design decision, subject to TOR-CONSTRAINT-001 and TOR-CONSTRAINT-003.

### TOR-NONGOAL-003 – treating branch-only symbols as compatibility-bound

The fact that the branch is ahead of the tag does not create a compatibility
obligation for symbols that have never appeared in a formal release tag. It
also does not erase one for the released function. The design must distinguish
the two cases per surface.

## Handoff

Readiness by scope:

- Ready for design classification: yes. The surface classification and the
  compatibility decision framework do not depend on the open items.
- Ready for implementation planning: no. Blocked on TOR-OPEN-001 to
  TOR-OPEN-004; the ExecPlan is a draft with those blockers recorded.
- Blocked items: the change contract, the commitment terms, the consumer
  profile, and the function's identity.

The design inherits TOR-CONSTRAINT-001 to TOR-CONSTRAINT-003, TOR-GOAL-001 and
TOR-GOAL-002, and TOR-ASSUMPTION-001 to TOR-ASSUMPTION-003. The ExecPlan
inherits the applicable subset and records its own traceability. This revision
does not authorise a shim; it records why compatibility or a deliberate
migration may be required, and leaves the mechanism to the design.

Feedback route: if implementation falsifies an assumption, record the evidence
here first, then impact-check the technical design and the ExecPlan before
treating any change as complete.
