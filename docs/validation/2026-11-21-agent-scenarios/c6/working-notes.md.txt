# Working notes: private API and storage reshape

Status: authoring working note, not a delivered document. Date 2026-11-21.
Evidence basis: isolation-limited intake; no repository was inspected.

## Evidence basis and limits

The supplied brief is the only project-specific input:

> Reshape a private API and storage representation; a deployed binary has
> already written records that must remain readable.

The authoring environment permitted reading only the installed
`terms-of-reference-doc`, `tech-design-doc` and `execplans` skills. No
repository, `docs/` directory, source file, test, fixture, ADR, release tag,
deployed record, roadmap, prior plan or PR was inspected. Consequently every
claim about the affected system is `[OPEN]` except the brief's own statements.
Nothing in this note or the derived drafts approves design or implementation.

One internal ambiguity is visible in the brief: "must remain readable" does not
name the reader. The plausible readings are the new binary reading old records,
the deployed binary reading records after a rollback, and long-term readability
of pre-migration backups. They impose different obligations and gate different
designs. The ambiguity is recorded as `TOR-OPEN-001`, not resolved by
assumption.

Conspicuously absent: evidence of the API's privacy and consumers, its release
lineage, the storage format, the storage backend's atomicity guarantees, record
volume, backup and retention policy, maintenance-window availability, corpus
provenance, approval authority, and the motivation for reshaping now.

## Elicitation queue

One question per turn, in dependency order. All remain unanswered because the
authoring environment has no interactive sponsor.

1. `TOR-OPEN-002` – Is the API genuinely application-internal, with no external
   consumer, no published package or ABI, and no other repository calling it?
   What is its release lineage? This question is first because a real external
   consumer changes the compatibility classification and stops the current
   plan.
2. `TOR-OPEN-001` – Which reader must "remain readable" protect: the new binary
   reading old records, the deployed binary reading new records after rollback,
   pre-migration backups for a retention period, or some combination?
3. `TOR-OPEN-003` – Is the reshape structural only, preserving every record's
   meaning and observable value, or does it change semantics, units, defaults
   or optionality?
4. `TOR-OPEN-004` – What storage backend is used, and what atomicity and
   durability guarantees does it document for replacing one record?
5. `TOR-OPEN-005` – Is a maintenance window with a single, stopped writer
   available for cutover?
6. `TOR-OPEN-009` – Do any other deployed readers, writers or peers share the
   records or a wire format?
7. `TOR-OPEN-007` – Can a corpus of records written by the deployed binary be
   captured, and at which versions?
8. `TOR-OPEN-006` – How long must pre-migration records and backups remain
   readable, and what backup retention applies?
9. `TOR-OPEN-008` – What are the record count, maximum record size and
   acceptable migration duration?
10. `TOR-OPEN-010` – Who approves the design and execution plan, and who
    accepts residual risk?
11. `TOR-OPEN-011` – Why is the reshape being requested now, and what
    deficiency does the current representation cause?

## Research summary

### Ecosystem survey

Not performed, deliberately. The brief selects no library, framework or
storage technology, and the design decisions depend on repository facts rather
than ecosystem facts. If intake identifies a specific backend, targeted
research into that backend's documented atomicity and durability semantics is
required before `EP-M0` can close.

### Standards and specifications

No applicable external standard was identified. The normative policy sources
are the installed skills.

### Key findings

1. Source-API compatibility machinery is prohibited for private APIs,
   application-internal public declarations, test-only APIs, pre-1.0 APIs and
   unreleased changes ahead of the latest formal release tag.
2. Persisted data and wire formats are a separate obligation. Deployed records
   may require a migration even when source compatibility is prohibited.
3. Every proposed compatibility layer must answer "compatible with whom or
   what?" and trace the answer to a real requirement. Otherwise it is
   compatibility theatre.
4. Milestones are coherent validated plateaus, not opportunities to keep old
   and new source interfaces alive in parallel.
5. Verification is designed with the implementation, with named invariants,
   lemmas, axioms, non-vacuity arguments and negative controls.

### Research gaps

The gaps are all intake gaps rather than literature gaps: baseline API, field
mapping, storage semantics, corpus provenance, retention and approval
authority. External research was not run; this is recorded so that no claim in
the drafts rests on unperformed research.
