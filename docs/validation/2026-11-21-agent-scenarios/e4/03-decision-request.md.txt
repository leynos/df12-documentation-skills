## 1. What happened

The live ExecPlan cites source symbol `<SYM>` and dependency contract `<DEP>` at
revision A. Revision B changes the contract and makes the planned failure path
`<F>` unreachable. The plan intended `<F>`, implemented in `<branch>` and

to contribute to milestone `<EP-Mn>` acceptance.

## 2. Why work stopped

1. The revision-A acceptance evidence is stale. A green result at revision A is
   not current evidence at revision B.
2. `<test-old>` can no longer enter the failure branch. Continuing to count it
   would produce a vacuous pass. Forcing the old failure through a mock or shim
   would assert a guarantee the dependency no longer provides.
3. `<DEP>` revision B is a dependency change from the plan's cited revision A.
   The plan does not record an authorization for B. If revision A was
   authorized, adopting B is a tolerance trigger; either way, it is not an
   implementation detail.
4. The change may falsify a technical-design requirement and, if the failure
   mode is recorded upstream, a terms-of-reference dependency record or
   assumption. Those need explicit revision before the plan can claim
   conformance.
5. The governing plan rules require the deviation to be recorded, the plan to
   enter `BLOCKED`, and implementation to wait for acceptance.

## 3. Decisions requested

1. Dependency authorization. Authorize `<DEP>` at the exact revision B
   identifier, superseding revision A, or retain revision A. If revision A is
   retained, record why it remains supported, secure, and permitted, and name
   the expiry owner and re-review date.
2. Failure-path disposition. Choose one:
   - Option A: adopt revision B, retire `<F>`, and withdraw `<VO-FAIL>` and
     `<test-old>` as acceptance evidence, replacing them with total-outcome
     mapping.
   - Option B: adopt revision B and re-source the requirement at a reachable
     boundary, with new acceptance evidence.
   - Option C: pin revision A and retain the failure path, accepting the
     support, security, and evidence-expiry risk.
   - Option D, preserving the old test through a mock, shim, ignored test, or
     expected failure, is rejected and is not offered.
3. Upstream revisions. Accept the proposed technical-design revision. Accept
   the proposed terms-of-reference dependency-record revision, and any
   assumption revision, if the trace check finds a load-bearing item. If the
   check finds none, record that finding explicitly.
4. Resumption. Accept the revised plan acceptance evidence and authorize
   resumption at `<EP-Mn>`. Design acceptance alone does not authorize
   implementation.

| Choice   | Effect on `<F>`                         | Evidence required before resuming                          | Principal risk                                      |
| -------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Option A | Retired with an accepted rationale      | Revision-B contract, trace showing no independent need, `VO-ERRMAP` | A user-visible guarantee is lost with the mechanism |
| Option B | Re-sourced at `<reachable boundary>`    | Revision-B contract, upstream requirement, new witness and negative control | Larger design and verification change               |
| Option C | Retained at revision A                  | Evidence that A remains supportable and permitted; expiry owner | Support, security, and stale-evidence exposure      |

Decision rule supplied for discussion: choose Option A when the failure
requirement was derived solely from revision A's contract; choose Option B when
it traces to an upstream goal, constraint, or success criterion that survives
the change; choose Option C only with positive supportability evidence.
